# Art Direction

## Memory and emotional register

The image recalls a summer afternoon seen from a high window: sunlight catches many tiny facets of a river, each briefly opening like a minute firework and then returning to water. The desired state is attentive stillness—not an effects showcase.

The water should feel materially present but visually recessive. The glimmers carry the gaze through uneven regions of activity while broad areas remain quiet.

## Visual vocabulary

- **Water:** low-saturation deep blue-gray and cyan-gray; subtle value variation; no pure black.
- **Light:** silver-white and warm white dominate; soft gold is rare and never orange or neon.
- **Shape:** mostly very small round or gently irregular points with crisp cores; fewer medium points and very rare larger flashes. Avoid repeated ellipses, crosses, and regular stars.
- **Distribution:** a directional, broken reflection band with holes, clusters, and sparse outskirts. Never a uniform particle field.
- **Motion:** evolving surface normals create local births, breaths, and extinctions. Positions feel anchored while intensity changes.
- **Rhythm:** many overlapping time scales; no global pulse, hard popping, obvious loop, or frame-to-frame noise.
- **Water clues:** broad tonal undulation and fine normal variation are sensed indirectly. Visible wave lines stay subordinate.

## Composition

The river fills the viewport. The primary reflection band can sit off-center and should have a tapered, irregular footprint. The brightest events are scarce enough to remain meaningful. Negative space is essential.

## Anti-examples

- Starfield, galaxy, aurora, magic dust, fireflies, glitter wallpaper, snowfall, meteor shower.
- Particle sprites that translate sideways or stream toward the camera.
- Uniform Poisson noise without a coherent reflection envelope.
- A tiled sine-wave grid, high-contrast contour lines, checker patterns, or liquid-chrome distortion.
- Strong cyan/magenta glow, clipped white fields, thick bloom halos, lens flare, chromatic aberration.
- Synchronized blinking or a short, recognizable pulse cycle.
- Pointer splashes, expanding rings, long trails, or rubber-sheet deformation.

## Phase 1 art acceptance focus

1. Are most glimmers perceived as staying in place while brightening and fading?
2. Does the image combine many tiny glints with only a few strong events?
3. Is the distribution clearly uneven and clustered, with calm water between regions?

Automatic checks can reject blank or structurally broken output, but final judgments on naturalism, restraint, and emotional fit require human review.

## Current visual-refactor target

- A dense foundation of sub-pixel and near-pixel glimmers should read as a broad field from afar, while local clustering remains visible up close.
- Medium points provide rhythm; rare bright cores provide focus. Only the rare bright layer earns a restrained halo.
- Point locations stay spatially stable. Their apparent life comes from independent compound temporal envelopes and changing normal response.
- Water should sit deeper than the lights: cool deep blue-gray with cyan-gray lift, slow low-frequency depth variation, and very subtle multi-directional surface texture.
